# Executive Narrative & Storyline

*Status: `scaffold` — see [`../../../skills_index.json`](../../../skills_index.json) and [`../../../meta/maturity_levels.md`](../../../meta/maturity_levels.md).*

## Purpose

Turns analysis into a narrative and storyline that serves executive decision-making.

## Anchored in research

- McKinsey — storyline thinking
- Well-known design-/business-design-consultancy storytelling competency models
- Minto, Barbara (1996), *The Pyramid Principle: Logic in Writing and
  Thinking* — the structural technique underlying McKinsey storyline
  thinking: state the answer first (the governing thought), support it with
  grouped, MECE arguments, and drive each argument down to its evidence. The
  SCQA opening (Situation–Complication–Question–Answer) frames why the
  audience should care before the answer lands.

## Method (draft — to be expanded)

1. **Start from the governing thought** — the single-sentence answer or
   recommendation the entire narrative has to support. If you can't state it
   in one sentence, the underlying analysis isn't finished yet.
2. **Open with SCQA**: Situation (the shared, uncontested starting point),
   Complication (what changed, or what's now at stake), Question (the
   question this naturally raises), Answer (the governing thought) — so the
   audience feels the need for the answer before they hear it.
3. **Group the supporting arguments into a MECE structure** beneath the
   governing thought — typically 3 to 5 arguments, each one a complete
   reason the governing thought is true, not a topic label.
4. **Order the arguments with one consistent logic** — deductively (premise
   → premise → conclusion) or inductively (grouped by shared reasoning, e.g.
   structural / financial / operational) — pick one and apply it throughout
   rather than mixing the two.
5. **Push every argument down to its evidence.** Each claim in the storyline
   should trace to a specific number, quote, or fact; anything that can't be
   traced gets marked `[assumption — verify]`.
6. **Build the storyline before the slides.** Write the narrative as a
   sequence of one-sentence headlines (a "flow"), and only then decide which
   headline needs which chart — a storyline that reads as a complete
   argument end-to-end without any slides is doing its job.

## What this skill does NOT do

- Doesn't make the final decision for you — it produces a structured draft to
  support a human decision.
- Doesn't confirm figures, market data, or competitor data from memory — it
  uses the inputs you provide, or marks an assumption clearly
  (`[assumption — verify]`).
- Doesn't do the visual design of the presentation materials — it structures
  the content and the narrative arc.
