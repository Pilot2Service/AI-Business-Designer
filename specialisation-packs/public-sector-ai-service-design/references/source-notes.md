# Source notes — what came from where

This pack was built from two kinds of material, and it matters which is
which.

## 1. Adapted from the owner's Pilot2Service commercialisation framework

The owner is the author and rights holder of the Pilot2Service
commercialisation framework (published toolkit, ISBN 978-952-94-9332-6,
copyright Adductor Magnus Oy, commercially sold at pilot2service.com — a
20-module planning workbook, a 3-sprint project model, and a 4-format
communication frame for taking a public-sector pilot to a
commercialisation decision).

Because the owner holds full rights, using this material is a business
choice, not a legal question. The choice made for this pack: adapt only
the genuinely public-sector-specific *principles*, rewritten at the level
of transferable method — not the source's exact worked questions, task
checklists, sprint structures, or deliverable templates, which remain the
paid product's differentiated content.

The one skill in this pack most directly grounded in the source is
`ps-decision-readiness-and-public-communication`. It generalizes a
six-element model for building decision-readiness in a public-sector
audience (found in the source's Communication Frame chapter, each element
paired with an explicit "Public Sector Specifics" callout: political and
social implications, public accountability and transparency, alignment
with policy mandates, budget-cycle-linked urgency, strategic alignment,
and the general dynamics of public-sector decision-making). The skill
keeps the six elements and the public-sector reasoning behind each one,
and drops the source's specific example language, deliverable-format
instructions (Executive Summary / Pitch Deck / Written Resolution
Proposal / full plan), and any AI-specific material — this skill applies
the model to AI proposals specifically, which the source does not.

Two smaller pieces of framing carried over the same way, credited here
rather than to a specific external framework:
- The observation that pilots serve a distinct evaluative function in the
  public sector — testing policy-aligned ideas without committing full
  budget, and needing outcomes read against policy goals, not just
  commercial traction — informs `ps-ai-opportunity-screening-for-public-value`.
- The recurring emphasis on regulatory and compliance review as an early,
  not late, gate (the source's Regulation & Compliance module) informs
  the "flag early" posture of `ps-regulatory-and-ethical-guardrails-for-public-ai`
  and `ps-procurement-and-public-funding-navigation`, though the specific
  compliance-assessment content in this pack is written fresh, not
  adapted, since the source's version is generic rather than
  public-sector-specific.

## 2. Written fresh for this pack

`ps-stakeholder-and-political-landscape-mapping`,
`ps-procurement-and-public-funding-navigation`,
`ps-regulatory-and-ethical-guardrails-for-public-ai`,
`ps-public-value-business-case-framing`, and
`ps-community-and-equity-impact-assessment` are not adapted from the
Pilot2Service source. They draw on general public administration,
public-value, and public procurement practice, and on this repo's own
established patterns for extending a core-pack skill (see how
`business-model-canvas`'s resilience-heuristics layer extends
`business-case-and-analysis` and `opportunity-recognition` — same
relationship here, aimed at public-sector-specific fit instead).

## What was deliberately held back

The full depth of the Pilot2Service source (20 planning modules with
sprint-level tasks and tools, the 3-sprint project model, and the 4
deliverable-format templates) is richer and more extensive than what's
in this pack — enough to justify its own, separate, standalone
specialisation pack focused specifically on taking a completed public
sector pilot through a commercialisation/exploitation decision. That is
a deliberate scope decision, not a gap: the owner chose to start with
this smaller, more broadly applicable "public sector AI service design"
lens and revisit the larger commercialisation-planning pack separately,
once its own scope and packaging (and how much of the source workbook's
structure to carry over vs. rewrite) has been worked out on its own
terms.
