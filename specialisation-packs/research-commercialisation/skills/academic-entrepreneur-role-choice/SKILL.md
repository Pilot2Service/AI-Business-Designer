# Academic Entrepreneur Role Choice

## Purpose

Choose your own role in a spin-out (full-time founder, scientific advisor,
part-time) and manage balancing an academic career with entrepreneurship.

## Based on

the owner's published commercialisation guide (owner, 2025). Chapter "The
Academic Entrepreneur's Path" (pp. 59-63).

- UK review (Independent Review of University Spin-out Companies): more than
  half of spin-out founders who left academia did not intend to return, 12%
  wanted to return, 31% were uncertain
- Typical consulting time allocation for academics: c. 20%/year (roughly one
  day a week or 30 days/year)

## Method

1. Identify three main models: (a) full-time founder/executive (leave of
   absence or resignation), (b) scientific advisor/shareholder while
   remaining in academia, (c) part-time hybrid in the early stage.
2. Assess the career risk openly: a full-time move can slow publication pace
   and make it harder to return to academia — ask about your organisation's
   leave-of-absence and tenure-clock policies.
3. If staying on as an advisor: find out the permitted consulting time (often
   c. 20%/year) and keep track of it — entrepreneurship often exceeds this
   in practice, which can lead to burnout.
4. Discuss with your department and TTO early: leave-of-absence options,
   continuity of thesis supervision, conflict-of-interest rules.
5. Formalise use of the university's/organisation's resources (lab space,
   student work) through agreements — avoid ambiguity about IP and working
   time.
6. Consider a consultant/freelancer route if the "product" is your expertise
   rather than a single piece of IP — this doesn't always require setting up
   a company.
7. Keep publication and IP protection coordinated: time the patent filing
   before publication, manage conflicts of interest transparently (e.g. an
   objective third party runs the clinical trial for your own invention).

## What this skill does NOT do

- Does not give a final employment-law or contractual interpretation — check
  your own organisation's practices and, if needed, an employment law
  specialist.
- Does not make the personal career decision for you.
