# Research Opportunity Recognition

## Purpose

Assess whether a research result has real commercial potential before
building a commercialisation strategy — distinguishing genuine market pull
from technology push.

## Based on

the owner's published commercialisation guide (owner, 2025). Chapter "Spotting
Real Opportunities" (pp. 18-20).

- Kirzner (1973/1979) — entrepreneurial alertness (see also the
  opportunity-recognition core pack)
- VC investor evaluation criteria: market signal often outweighs the science
  alone

## Method

1. State the problem in a single sentence: what problem does the invention
   solve, and for whom?
2. Map the current alternatives / status quo — why do they fall short?
3. Do a value proposition mapping and competitor analysis: what is the genuine
   edge (price, performance, new capability)?
4. Test early: customer interviews, surveys, a landing page, proof-of-market
   funding.
5. Assess TRL and freedom to operate side by side — is the idea at a point
   where a prototype is possible without unreasonable cost or regulatory
   barriers?
6. Assess timing and the competitive landscape — look for the "sweet spot":
   not too early, not into a crowded market without differentiation.
7. Treat the whole assessment as a testable hypothesis: strong signals
   (enthusiastic industry partners, positive user feedback, clear market gaps)
   → move forward aggressively; weak signals → redirect or preserve resources.

## What this skill does NOT do

- Does not make the final go/no-go decision for you.
- Does not assess patentability in a legal sense — see
  `ip-disclosure-and-ownership-check` and your own TTO/IP counsel.
- Does not replace real customer conversations — it structures what to ask and
  how to interpret the answers.
