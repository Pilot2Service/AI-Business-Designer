# Case examples: spin-outs in practice

Source: the owner's published commercialisation guide (2025), chapter "Case
Examples: Global Spin-outs in Action" (pp. 40-44). Used as a reference by the
`industry-specific-commercialisation-playbook`, `spinout-vs-licensing-pathway`,
and `funding-pathway-design` skills.

## Oxford Nanopore Technologies (UK)

From Oxford lab science to a £5 billion listed company with portable DNA
sequencing. Emerged from Professor Hagan Bayley's laboratory at the
University of Oxford, founded in 2005. Over 15 years from lab to market, over
£800 million raised along the way. The MinION handheld sequencer has
revolutionised genomic analysis; listed on the London Stock Exchange in 2021,
valued at c. £5bn. Shows that deep science requires patience, strong IP, and
sustained university support — but can deliver global impact. nanoporetech.com

## Milk. Inc. (Japan)

Tokyo researchers combining AI and hyperspectral imaging for next-generation
cancer diagnostics. Founded in Tokyo in 2019; developed a hyperspectral
imaging system for pathology and is building the "ANSWER for Pathology"
diagnostic product. Still at an early stage, but has received recognition and
awards supporting its growth. Shows that combining advanced imaging hardware
with AI can be resource-intensive but uniquely well positioned for critical
clinical needs. milk-med.com

## OrganOx (UK)

Transforming organ transplantation with a device that keeps donor organs
alive — acquired for $1.5bn. Another Oxford spin-out, Professors Constantin
Coussios and Peter Friend, combining engineering and surgical expertise.
Founded in 2008; went through several funding rounds before the Japanese
medical device company Terumo acquired it in 2025 for $1.5bn. Shows that
mission-driven healthcare ventures often require long regulatory pathways,
but when the clinical value is clear they can achieve major global exits.
organox.com

## Cyclize (Germany)

Turning plastic waste and CO₂ into syngas with a plasma process — spun out
of Stuttgart. Emerged from the University of Stuttgart in 2022, when four
researchers decided to commercialise their plasma-based process for
converting mixed plastic waste and CO₂ into syngas. Raised €4.75 million in
seed funding in 2024 and used Germany's EXIST Research Transfer programme.
Has built pilot plants and is progressing towards industrial scale. Shows how
public funding and solid technical validation can together help a promising
environmental technology move from lab to industry. cyclize.de

## SimYog Technology (India)

Virtual EMI labs from IISc Bangalore, cutting design costs in semiconductors
and electronics. Founded in 2017 in Bangalore, out of the Indian Institute of
Science (IISc). Under Professor Dipanjan Gope, the team combined academic and
industrial expertise to create a simulation-based tool letting electronics
designers test electromagnetic compatibility virtually, reducing costly
redesigns. Seed funding from Bosch and Ideaspring Capital, plus a $2.5 million
Series A. Shows how software and simulation spin-outs can solve urgent
problems and gain traction quickly. simyog.com

## BoobyBiome (UK)

A women-founded UCL spin-out using breast milk microbiome data to improve
infant health. Spun out of University College London in 2019, founded by a
team of women researchers following five years of breast milk microbiome
research. Raised £2.5 million in seed funding in 2025 to expand its work,
building the world's largest breast milk microbiome dataset and developing
infant health products. Shows how strong scientific data and diverse founding
teams can attract investors even in heavily regulated fields such as
biotechnology. boobybiome.com

## Twelve (USA)

Born at Lawrence Berkeley Lab, turning CO₂ into fuels and materials — raised
$200 million. California-based Twelve was founded in 2015 out of research at
Lawrence Berkeley National Lab and the Cyclotron Road programme. The
researcher-founders developed a process for converting CO₂ into fuels and
materials. Has raised over $200 million from venture investors and public
grants, and is running commercial pilots with NASA and major industrial
players. Shows how US national laboratories and accelerator programmes can
serve as launchpads for world-class climate technologies. twelve.co

## General observations (handbook summary)

- Spin-outs emerge everywhere — Europe, Asia, the US, emerging markets — and
  across every field of science from biotech to AI and sustainability.
- Team composition is as critical as the underlying science. Complementary
  skills (engineering + business) predict survival.
- Funding journeys differ: deep tech often requires patient capital;
  digital and simulation tools can scale faster.
- Exits vary: IPOs, acquisitions, or sustained growth — all are valid
  pathways depending on industry and ambition.
- Ecosystem support — TTOs, incubators, public grants, industry partners —
  appears consistently in success stories.
