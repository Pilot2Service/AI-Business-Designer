# Human-AI Collaboration Design — shared guardrails

General guardrails (disclaimer, no fabricated numbers, premise-checking,
keeping maturity as an internal note) are collected in one place: **see
[`../meta/shared-guardrails.md`](../meta/shared-guardrails.md) — read that
first.** This file only contains what's genuinely specific to this pack.

---

## This pack is the deep layer, not a replacement

Two other skills in this repo already touch human-AI oversight at a lighter
level: `specialisation-packs/ai-native-startup-design/skills/closed-loop-process-and-human-oversight-design`
(a simple in-the-loop / on-the-loop / outside-the-loop model for early-stage
process design) and `ai-strategy-and-governance/skills/ai-output-curation-and-quality-control`
(the same three-tier model applied to content quality control). Neither is
replaced by this pack. Use those two when a process or product is still
early and a simple three-way split is enough. Reach for this pack once the
process is live enough to need an operational maturity model, a
confidence-score routing rule, a named accountable calibration owner, and a
metric that tells you whether oversight is actually working — not just
declared.

## Don't present a maturity level as an achievement to announce

A team can *say* it has reached "Collaborative Intelligence" (Level 3)
while its own override-rate data quietly reveals Level 0 automation bias.
This pack's skills are built to be self-checking: `hitl-maturity-and-
confidence-routing` and `hitl-override-metrics-and-feedback-audit` exist
specifically to catch the gap between the maturity level an organization
claims and the one its own usage data shows.

## Sourcing note — treat named attributions in this pack carefully

Several of this pack's grounding concepts come from named 2026
practitioners whose specific quotes could not be independently verified
word-for-word at the time these skills were written (Su Belagodu, Vitaly
Friedman, Jasmine Orange, Joshua Ebner — real, identifiable people whose
general area of work matches the attributed idea, but whose exact wording
is unconfirmed), and one attribution from the source material ("Curt
Strovink") could not be verified as an identifiable person at all and was
dropped in favor of the independently well-documented underlying concept
instead. Each skill's "Anchored in research" section states exactly which
parts are confirmed, which are attributed-with-caveat, and which were
re-grounded in an independent source. Don't upgrade a caveated attribution
to a confirmed one without re-verifying it yourself.
